# Receiver Reliance and Jev: publication note

Supplement DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.22783171](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22783171).

Prepared and collected September 15, 2026 Pacific; UTC timestamps cross into September 16. Version: `2026-09-15-jev-followup`. Creator: James Dimachkie.

Two exploratory follow-ups tested a live TypeSafe Jev semantic checker inside Receiver Reliance (RR) and an equally informed conventional receiving policy. The synthetic feasibility pilot comprised 48 episodes from six hand-authored families and 30 unique live judgments. The fixed VitaminC follow-up used 120 real-revision contrast pairs from 120 pages, 240 live judgments and 480 correlated workflow replays. All responses returned jev-1.13.0 through jev-latest.

Semantic checking combined with structural receiving checks withheld every unsupported release on the synthetic fixtures under either receiving policy; the checker remains fallible, no answer generation or repair was tested, and reliable autonomous operation is not established.

RR and the competent conventional policy made identical release decisions in every episode of both pilots. On the benchmark, support probability decreased in 112/120 pairs and three-way benchmark-label agreement was 194/240 (80.8%). The primary gate accepted one benchmark-negative example and withheld 38 benchmark-positive examples. Source/label conflicts and omitted page-title context prevent interpreting these counts as clean model accuracy or all withheld positives as valid work lost. The sole negative-label acceptance involves unresolved Heinsberg city/district wording, not an unequivocally demonstrated false factual release.

The result is no incremental RR release-decision benefit in these narrow honest-host contracts. Engineering, maintenance, audit, coordination and field value were not measured. The evidence neither establishes broad equivalence nor shows RR to be worthless. These distinct populations must not be pooled with each other or the original 1,805-episode study.
